## Core paper explanation

PaLI-3 studies a compact vision-language system with a strong SigLIP-pretrained image encoder and an encoder–decoder language model. It separates visual pretraining, multimodal learning, resolution scaling, and task-specific transfer.

## Execution flow

Follow the data through the mechanism. Training objectives and inference behavior are distinguished in the linked explanations.

### Pretrain visual alignment

For normalized embeddings $v_i,t_j$, a pair logit is $s_{ij}=a v_i^\top t_j+b$. With pair label $z_{ij}\in\{-1,1\}$,
$$L_{\mathrm{pair}}=\log(1+e^{-z_{ij}s_{ij}}).$$
This is the SigLIP mechanism underlying the visual encoder, not a box-localization objective.

### Train multimodal tasks

The first multimodal stage keeps the image encoder frozen and trains on a task mixture:
$$L=-\mathbb E_{(I,x,y)}\sum_t\log p_\theta(y_t\mid y_{<t},I,x).$$
$x$ is the task prompt and $y$ the target text or structured sequence. OCR-rich and object-aware data matter because visual-language quality is not determined by image classification accuracy alone.

### Increase resolution and transfer

For square input resolution $R$ and patch width $P$, token count scales as $N\approx(R/P)^2$. Full visual self-attention has quadratic token-pair cost, so increasing $R$ can sharply raise memory and latency. PaLI-3 unfreezes the whole model during resolution increase, then freezes its image encoder for the reported task-transfer stage.

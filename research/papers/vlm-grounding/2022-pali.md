## Core paper explanation

PaLI combines a pretrained vision transformer with a multilingual encoder–decoder language model. Many vision-language tasks share a text-in/text-out interface, allowing a mixture of captioning, question answering, OCR, and other objectives.

## Execution flow

### Encode image and prompt

Let image features be $V\in\mathbb R^{N\times d_v}$ and prompt embeddings $E\in\mathbb R^{T\times d}$. A projection aligns widths before the multimodal encoder:
$$H=\operatorname{Encoder}([VW;E]),\qquad W\in\mathbb R^{d_v\times d}.$$
This compact notation expresses the interface, not every positional embedding or normalization in PaLI.

### Decode task targets

The decoder models
$$p(y\mid I,x)=\prod_{t=1}^{|y|}p(y_t\mid y_{<t},H).$$
Task prompts determine the requested output. A caption, an OCR transcript, and a visual-question answer share a sequence interface but require different target preparation and evaluation.

### Mix training tasks

A useful expression for mixture training is
$$L=\sum_k w_k\,\mathbb E_{(I,x,y)\sim D_k}\left[-\log p_\theta(y\mid I,x)\right],\quad \sum_k w_k=1.$$
Here $D_k$ denotes a task dataset and $w_k$ its sampling probability. This makes the mixture explicit without inventing equal weights. PaLI studies joint scaling of vision and language capacity.

## Core paper explanation

BLIP combines image–text retrieval, pair matching, and caption generation in a shared encoder–decoder design. Its CapFilt pipeline improves training data by generating captions and filtering both generated and original text.

## Execution flow

Follow the data through the mechanism. Training objectives and inference behavior are distinguished in the linked explanations.

### Align and match

Global image/text embeddings support contrastive learning. An image-grounded text encoder also predicts whether a pair matches. For binary label $y$ and matching probability $p$,
$$L_{\mathrm{ITM}}=-y\log p-(1-y)\log(1-p).$$
BLIP uses hard negatives and momentum-derived targets for its contrastive branch; the linked exercise isolates the simpler symmetric alignment kernel.

### Generate captions

Replacing bidirectional text self-attention with causal attention enables generation. For caption tokens $w_{1:T}$ and image $I$,
$$L_{\mathrm{LM}}=-\sum_{t=1}^T\log p_\theta(w_t\mid w_{<t},I).$$
Only preceding text is available to the next-token prediction. Encoder and decoder share selected parameters, including cross-attention and feed-forward layers.


**Planned component problems:** Combine Alignment and Caption Losses. These are planned work, not runnable links; titles without a reviewed blueprint remain unreviewed ideas.

### Bootstrap training data

CapFilt constructs synthetic captions and rejects mismatched pairs from both text sources. A teaching view of the retained set is
$$D'=\{(I,c):c\in\{c_{\mathrm{web}},c_{\mathrm{synthetic}}\},\ f_{\mathrm{match}}(I,c)=1\}.$$
This set expression describes the data decision, not an alternative numeric filter threshold. The captioner and filter are fine-tuned from the pretrained model before bootstrapping.

## Why this matters on a robot

A robot dataset can benefit from better observation captions, but synthetic descriptions can hallucinate state or actions. Keep original sensor records, caption provenance, and rejected examples for audit.

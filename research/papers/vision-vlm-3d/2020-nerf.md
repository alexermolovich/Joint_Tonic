## Agent brief

Use NeRF to connect camera geometry, sampling, positional encoding, neural fields, and differentiable rendering. Exercises should isolate the rendering equation before asking learners to train a scene model.

## Contribution

NeRF represents a static scene as a continuous function from 3D position and viewing direction to density and view-dependent color. Images supervise the field through differentiable alpha compositing along calibrated camera rays.

## Prerequisite concepts

- Pinhole camera intrinsics/extrinsics and rigid transforms
- Ray parameterization and numerical integration
- Multilayer perceptrons and sinusoidal positional encoding
- Probability-style transmittance and alpha compositing

## Method map

1. Generate world-space rays from image pixels and known camera poses.
2. Sample coarse 3D points between near/far bounds, with stratified perturbation during training.
3. Encode coordinates and view direction with multiple sinusoidal frequencies.
4. Predict density from position and color from features plus direction.
5. Convert density to alpha, accumulate transmittance, and composite color.
6. Resample important regions hierarchically for a fine rendering pass.

## Essential equations

For ordered samples along a ray, define

$$
\alpha_i=1-e^{-\sigma_i\Delta_i},\qquad
T_i=\prod_{j<i}(1-\alpha_j).
$$

The rendered color is the differentiable weighted sum

$$
\hat C(r)=\sum_i T_i\alpha_i c_i.
$$

The same weights give expected depth, 

$$
\hat D(r)=\sum_i T_i\alpha_i t_i.
$$

## Visual teaching plan

- **Ray marcher:** Drag density peaks along one ray and watch alpha, transmittance, sample weights, color, and expected depth update.
- **Camera geometry scene:** Move a virtual camera around a coordinate frame and display the rays for selected pixels.
- **Coarse-to-fine sampler:** Show a coarse weight histogram, its cumulative distribution, and fine samples drawn by inverse-CDF sampling.

## Data and evaluation

The paper evaluates synthetic scenes with known poses and forward-facing real captures, using PSNR, SSIM, and perceptual similarity for held-out views. For robotics, also measure depth consistency, pose sensitivity, render latency, and failure under dynamic objects.

## Limitations and failure modes

- Optimization is scene-specific and originally expensive.
- Assumes a mostly static, consistently illuminated scene and accurate poses.
- Photorealistic view synthesis does not guarantee metric geometry or semantic correctness.
- Near/far bounds and coordinate conventions are easy sources of silent error.

## Implementation decomposition

1. Camera-ray construction with coordinate-frame tests.
2. Stratified sampling and near/far validation.
3. Positional encoding.
4. Density-to-alpha and transmittance computation.
5. RGB/depth/opacity rendering.
6. PDF-based hierarchical sampling and a tiny-scene training loop.

## Problem blueprints

### Generate world-space camera rays

- **Type:** 3D geometry
- **Difficulty:** Medium
- **Inputs:** Pixel grid, intrinsic matrix, camera-to-world transform
- **Outputs:** Per-pixel ray origins and unit directions
- **Grading:** Analytic camera cases; coordinate convention; normalization; batch shapes; no pixel loops

### Volume-render sampled colors

- **Type:** Differentiable tensor algorithm
- **Difficulty:** Hard
- **Inputs:** Ordered sample depths, densities, RGB values, ray directions
- **Outputs:** Rendered RGB, expected depth, opacity, and sample weights
- **Grading:** Reference compositing; exclusive cumulative product; terminal interval handling; stable finite gradients

### Hierarchical inverse-CDF sampling

- **Type:** Sampling algorithm
- **Difficulty:** Hard
- **Inputs:** Bin edges, detached coarse weights, requested sample count, deterministic/random mode
- **Outputs:** Fine sample depths
- **Grading:** Correct empirical distribution; zero-weight fallback; sorted bounds; deterministic reproducibility

## MLOps and deployment extensions

- Treat camera calibration, pose convention, scene bounds, and image preprocessing as versioned data artifacts.
- Log ray-batch throughput, samples per ray, PSNR by camera region, and GPU memory.
- Detect input drift through pose coverage, blur, exposure, and dynamic-pixel estimates.
- For robot use, gate releases on geometry/depth consistency and worst-view latency, not rendered appearance alone.

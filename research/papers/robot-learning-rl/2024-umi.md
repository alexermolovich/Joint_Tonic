## Core paper explanation

Universal Manipulation Interface collects demonstrations with a handheld gripper and transfers them to robot policies through a shared sensing/action interface. Relative trajectories and latency matching reduce dependence on one fixed robot installation.

## Execution flow

Follow the data through the mechanism. Training objectives and inference behavior are distinguished in the linked explanations.

### Capture handheld demonstrations

Pose tracking supplies gripper transforms $T_{WG,t}$ in a common world frame. Camera-to-gripper calibration and gripper-width measurements connect visual observations to manipulation geometry. Human wrist motion is not automatically a feasible robot joint trajectory.


**Runnable component:** [Apply an SE(3) Transform to Batched Points](/problems/se3-transform-points). Practice expressing points in the demonstration’s calibrated tool/world frames; this does not estimate the calibration.

**Planned exercise:** Validate a Demonstration Replay Bundle.


### Represent relative action chunks

A relative pose target is
$$\Delta T_{t,k}=T_{WG,t}^{-1}T_{WG,t+k}.$$
Translation is in meters and rotation uses a declared representation. Relative trajectories reduce reliance on a global collection frame; they do not remove robot kinematic limits or collision constraints.


**Planned exercise:** Transform Demonstrations into a Shared Tool Frame.


### Match timing at deployment

For observation time $t_o$ and action execution time $t_e$, effective delay is $\delta=t_e-t_o$. Latency matching selects/timestamps the appropriate future action rather than executing an already stale chunk prefix. Bimanual control additionally requires consistent timing and relative frames across grippers.


**Planned exercise:** Align Camera and Gripper Timestamps.

## Why this matters on a robot

Portable demonstrations can broaden data collection beyond robot labs. Tracking drift, tool-camera calibration, and mismatched execution latency can break transfer even when the demonstrated motion looks correct.

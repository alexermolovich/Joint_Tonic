## Core paper explanation

R3M pretrains reusable visual representations on human video using temporal contrast, language alignment, and sparsity. The representation can then be frozen while a robot policy learns from comparatively few demonstrations.

## Execution flow

Follow the data through the mechanism. Training objectives and inference behavior are distinguished in the linked explanations.

### Learn temporal structure

For frames $i<j<k$, embeddings $z=F(I)$, and similarity $S(z,z')=-\|z-z'\|_2$, the temporal contrastive term has the form
$$L_{\mathrm{TC}}=-\log\frac{e^{S(z_i,z_j)}}{e^{S(z_i,z_j)}+e^{S(z_i,z_k)}+e^{S(z_i,z_{\mathrm{other}})}}.$$
The unrelated embedding comes from another video. Sampling order is part of the supervision.


**Implement this component:** [Sample Time-Contrastive Video Triplets](/problems/r3m-time-contrastive-video-triplets).

The exercise consumes recorded unit-interval draws and uses a declared sequential sampler. It preserves temporal order and different-video negatives, but does not claim a uniform distribution over every possible triple or the paper's exact sampling procedure.


### Align progress with language

A learned score $G(z_0,z_t,\ell)$ evaluates language-compatible visual progress. Its contrastive positive is a later state paired with the correct language; earlier progress and another video's progress supply negatives. The [v3 paper's equation 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.12601v3) combines temporal contrast, video-language alignment, and both L1 and L2 representation norms:

$$L=\lambda_tL_{\mathrm{TC}}+\lambda_lL_{\mathrm{lang}}+\lambda_1\|z\|_1+\lambda_2\|z\|_2.$$

Here the nonnegative coefficients control the four terms, and z is the visual embedding being regularized. The L2 term is a norm, not its square. Language alignment models progress rather than CLIP's image-caption matching objective. A component implementation must state its batch reduction, regularized representations and weights; these choices affect numerical equivalence to training.


**Implement this component:** [Combine Temporal and Language Alignment Losses](/problems/r3m-temporal-language-objective).

This component computes negative-Euclidean temporal scores, contrastive loss from supplied language-progress logits, and anchor L1/L2 norms. Its valid-row mean reduction and explicit weights make normalization inspectable; image encoding, the learned language scorer and optimizer remain outside this exercise.


### Train a robot policy

A downstream policy receives $z_t=F(I_t)$ and robot state, then learns the embodiment's actions. Human-video pretraining supplies representations, not joint-action labels. Evaluate against equal robot-demonstration budgets and held-out scene conditions.


**Implement this component:** [Evaluate Frozen Features in a Small Policy](/problems/r3m-frozen-feature-policy-step).

The bounded policy exercise performs one affine supervised update on supplied frozen visual features and robot state. It keeps held-out action labels out of gradients and reports action MSE. The paper's BatchNorm/MLP architecture, long training schedule and online rollout success evaluation are not reproduced by this step.

## Why this matters on a robot

Human activity can teach useful object and interaction features. Human camera viewpoints and motion statistics differ from robot observations, so a strong pretrained encoder may still miss contact-critical detail.

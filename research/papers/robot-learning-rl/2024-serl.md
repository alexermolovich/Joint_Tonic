## Core paper explanation

SERL packages practical components for sample-efficient real-robot reinforcement learning: visual off-policy learning, reward specification, reset strategies, and suitable control interfaces. Its contribution is a usable system integration rather than a claim of one new universal RL algorithm.

## Execution flow

Follow the data through the mechanism. Training objectives and inference behavior are distinguished in the linked explanations.

### Define control and reward

Actions pass through a declared controller, often designed for compliant contact. Reward can come from a binary classifier, an adversarially updated model, or robot-state logic. A classifier probability $p(\mathrm{success}\mid o)$ needs validation on policy-generated failures, not only curated positives.


**Planned exercise:** Validate Success-Classifier Rewards on Robot Failures.


### Run asynchronous collection and learning

The critic core is $L_Q=\mathbb E_D[(Q(s,a)-r-\gamma(1-d)V(s'))^2]$, with the actual algorithm supplying its target details and regularization. Actor/learner separation reduces idle robot time; policy versions and observation/action timing remain traceable.


**Planned exercise:** Merge Online and Demonstration Replay.

**Planned exercise:** Schedule Actor-Learner Updates.


### Reset and evaluate repeatedly

Forward and reset policies solve different objectives. Reset success must return the system to the intended initial-state distribution; otherwise later trials are not comparable. Record reset time and failures alongside training interaction time.


**Planned exercise:** Record Intervention and Reset Statistics.

## Why this matters on a robot

Reliable reward, reset, and controller interfaces often determine whether RL works outside simulation. Reward hacking, reset drift, and asynchronous timing mistakes are system failures even when the loss implementation is correct.

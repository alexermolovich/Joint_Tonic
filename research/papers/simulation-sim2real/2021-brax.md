## Core paper explanation

Brax expresses rigid-body simulation and learning in an accelerator-friendly JAX workflow. Vectorization, compilation, and automatic differentiation allow many environments and policy updates to remain on the same device.

## Execution flow

### Express a functional simulation step

Write $s_{t+1}=F(s_t,a_t;\theta)$, where $\theta$ includes model and solver settings. Explicit state supports batching and reproducibility. The original paper's engine should not be assumed identical to every later Brax physics backend.

### Batch and compile rollouts

For $B$ environments, $S\in\mathbb R^{B\times d_s}$ and $A\in\mathbb R^{B\times d_a}$ share the batch axis. A rollout stores $T\times B$ transitions; reset masks must affect only completed environments. Compilation and device-transfer time should be reported separately from steady-state stepping.


**Component practice**

- Vectorize Environment Resets — unreviewed idea; contract and evaluator required.

### Optimize policies

Direct differentiation follows
$$\frac{ds_{t+1}}{d\phi}=F_s\frac{ds_t}{d\phi}+F_a\frac{da_t}{d\phi},$$
for policy parameters $\phi$. Contact discontinuities and long-horizon sensitivity can make these gradients difficult; differentiability does not guarantee a useful optimization signal. The paper also benchmarks gradient-free-through-physics RL methods.

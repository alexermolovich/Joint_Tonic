## Core paper explanation

RAFT estimates optical flow by repeatedly updating one dense flow field using correlations between every pair of image features. It retains a high-resolution field rather than predicting flow only through a coarse-to-fine cascade. A recurrent update block consults a correlation pyramid around the current correspondence estimate and proposes a correction.

## Execution flow

Encode two frames, build and query all-pairs correlations, then iteratively update pixel correspondences.

### Build all-pairs correlations

For $F_1,F_2\in\mathbb R^{H\times W\times d}$,

$$
C_{ijkl}=\sum_{c=1}^{d}F_{1,ijc}F_{2,klc},
\qquad C\in\mathbb R^{H\times W\times H\times W}.
$$

The first spatial pair identifies a source location; the second identifies its possible destination. RAFT pools the destination dimensions to build its correlation pyramid while retaining the source grid. Pooling all four axes would change the lookup contract.

### Query around current matches

Let source pixel $p=(x,y)$ and flow estimate $u_k(p)$ point to destination $p'=p+u_k(p)$. At pyramid level $\ell$, query offsets around $p'/2^\ell$:

$$
g_k(p)=\operatorname{concat}_{\ell,\delta}
\mathcal B(C^\ell_p,p'/2^\ell+\delta).
$$

The sampled correlation features depend on the current estimate. Distinguish pixel units at input resolution, feature-grid resolution, and each pyramid level. Bilinear lookup permits noninteger correspondence estimates.


**Planned component problems:** Sample a correlation pyramid around flow estimates. These are planned work, not runnable links; titles without a reviewed blueprint remain unreviewed ideas.

### Iteratively refine the flow

A compact update is

$$
h_{k+1}=\operatorname{GRU}(h_k,g_k,u_k,c),
\qquad u_{k+1}=u_k+\Delta u_\theta(h_{k+1}).
$$

Here $c$ is first-image context and $k$ is a refinement iteration, not video time. Training supervises a sequence of intermediate flow estimates. For evaluation, endpoint error compares predicted and target displacement vectors in the same pixel units.

## Why this matters on a robot

Optical flow measures image displacement, not metric 3D velocity by itself. Occlusion, rolling shutter, moving cameras, and textureless regions complicate interpretation. A robot needs calibration and timing to connect flow to motion, and should identify pixels where correspondences are unreliable.

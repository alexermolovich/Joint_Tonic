## Core paper explanation

π0.5 combines heterogeneous robot and non-robot data with hierarchical subtask prediction and continuous action generation. It aims to transfer visual-language knowledge and robot skills to unfamiliar environments, rather than only adapting to one fixed workstation.

## Execution flow

Follow the data through the mechanism. Training objectives and inference behavior are distinguished in the linked explanations.

### Learn from heterogeneous data

For data sources $D_k$, a conceptual mixed objective is $L=\sum_k w_k\mathbb E_{D_k}[L_k]$. The source-specific targets differ: text and tokenized actions support autoregressive learning, while continuous action training uses an action expert. The equation exposes sampling weights without asserting equal weights or a single universal loss.

### Predict a subtask

At inference, the hierarchy is
$$p(A,m\mid o,\ell)=p(m\mid o,\ell)\,p(A\mid o,\ell,m),$$
where $m$ is a high-level subtask and $A\in\mathbb R^{H\times d}$ an action chunk. The subtask creates an intermediate semantic interface before motor prediction.

### Generate continuous control

Using an explicit noise-to-data teaching convention, $A_\tau=(1-\tau)E+\tau A$ with $E\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ gives
$$L_{\mathrm{flow}}=\mathbb E\|v_\theta(A_\tau,\tau,c)-(A-E)\|_F^2.$$
Integrate from noise to actions using the matching time/sign convention. The actual released implementation's parameterization must be checked before substituting this notation. Restore physical action units after sampling.

## Why this matters on a robot

Generalization must be assessed on genuinely unseen environments and object arrangements. A sensible subtask can still fail through low-level contact dynamics or perception errors; record both hierarchy decisions and execution outcomes.

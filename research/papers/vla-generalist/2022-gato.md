## Core paper explanation

Gato casts diverse text, visual, and embodied tasks into sequences processed by one causal transformer. Modality-specific tokenization and carefully masked output supervision make a shared model possible without pretending every input has the same semantics.

## Execution flow

Follow the data through the mechanism. Training objectives and inference behavior are distinguished in the linked explanations.

### Serialize observations and actions

Observations precede a separator and then actions at each agent timestep. Continuous values are mapped into a declared normalized range, with mu-law encoding where appropriate, and quantized into $1024$ bins. For $u\in[-1,1]$, a teaching uniform-bin map is
$$q=\min(1023,\lfloor1024(u+1)/2\rfloor).$$
Do not substitute this for every modality: image patches use learned embeddings and text uses its tokenizer.

### Train masked prediction

For sequence $x$ and output mask $m_t$,
$$L=-\frac{\sum_t m_t\log p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t})}{\sum_t m_t}.$$
The mask selects action/text targets. Position and modality encodings distinguish otherwise similar numeric tokens. Incorrect packing can leak an action into its own conditioning context.

### Decode an action sequence

At inference, append the current observation and generate the action tokens expected by that task. The decoder must restore the correct joint order, scale, and control mode. A torque in newton-meters and a joint position in radians cannot share a denormalizer simply because both were scalar tokens.
